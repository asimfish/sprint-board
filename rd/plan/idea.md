## Problem Statement

Fast visuomotor policies for long-horizon manipulation, including large pretrained vision-language-action models, fail on a substantial share of task configurations. A slow "System 2" — a planner with a failure detector that can take over control for a short stretch and then hand it back — can rescue many of these episodes at deployment time, but it is slow and costly to keep in the loop. It is not known whether, and through what mechanism, the trajectories produced under such takeovers can be distilled back into the fast "System 1" policy so that the fast policy becomes better on its own and needs the slow system less. Existing interactive imitation learning treats interventions as local action corrections from human experts and evaluates mostly on the training distribution; existing hierarchical systems keep the slow system permanently in the loop; and directly fine-tuning a large pretrained policy on a small set of corrections tends to disturb its ordinary behaviour.

## Core Hypothesis

We hypothesize that the value of System-2 takeovers to System 1 lies mainly in coverage rather than in data volume: takeovers convert exactly those task configurations that System 1 cannot complete into successful demonstrations, and combining these with System 1's own successful episodes — which supply consistent, complete executions of the nominal behaviour — yields a policy that generalizes better to unseen configurations than either source alone or than more of System 1's own successes. We further hypothesize that a System 1 trained this way internalizes part of System 2's competence and therefore triggers fewer takeovers when System 2 remains available, and that for large pretrained policies this distillation should be done by parameter isolation — a frozen base policy plus a separately trained corrector and a learned gate that predicts when System 2 would have taken over — rather than by fine-tuning the base weights.

## Proposed Methodology (High-Level Technical Approach)

We will run the fast policy under a slow supervisor that monitors the episode with cheap, verifiable checks (a bowl dropped, an empty grasp, an object tipped, no progress, running late, arms not returned) and, when failure is imminent, discards the remaining action chunk, executes a short corrective skill, and returns control. We will curate the resulting successful episodes by removing the student behaviour immediately preceding each takeover and any corrective segment that itself failed, and pool them with the fast policy's own successful episodes. We will train a fresh visuomotor diffusion policy on the pooled data and compare it, on held-out configurations and with paired starts, against policies trained on native data only (at matched episode count and at matched total volume) and on guided data only, so that coverage, complementarity and volume can be separated. We will measure the slow system's workload — number of takeovers and number of teacher-controlled steps — when each trained student is run under the same supervisor. We will iterate the loop, letting the supervisor guide the newly trained student and aggregating its guided episodes, so that guidance fades as the student improves. For a large pretrained policy we will keep its weights frozen and add a lightweight gate, trained to predict the supervisor's takeover decisions from the fast policy's own observations, together with a corrector trained on the supervisor's corrective segments, which acts only when the gate fires.

## Expected Contribution

1. Evidence that slow-system takeovers improve a fast policy primarily by covering configurations it cannot reach, separated from data volume and from data source by matched controls.
2. A measurable notion of how much a fast policy still depends on its slow supervisor, and evidence that distillation reduces this dependence without lowering system success.
3. A characterization of why weight-space fine-tuning of a pretrained vision-language-action policy on corrections trades ordinary competence for corrective competence, and a parameter-isolation alternative that keeps the pretrained policy intact.
4. An iterative protocol in which slow-system guidance fades as the fast policy improves.
